What Salesforce Learned After Using Employee Agent for a Year - Salesforce Skip to Content Skip to Footer 0% Bureaucracy and paperwork can waste a lot of time for employees. They want a key piece of information about a PTO policy or they want to know how to book space for an event, and that can trigger a frustrating hunt through outdated bookmarks and a slew of app logins. Then the dreaded moment: filing a ticket. So a year ago, we introduced Employee Agent internally to make it easy for our employees to quickly find accurate, trusted answers to HR-related questions. Today, Employee Agent is the most-used agent across Salesforce, with a 97.7% self-service rate, meaning nearly all sessions end with the employee resolving their own issue. No ticket-filing necessary. The Employee Agent handled over 300,000 conversations in its first year, from questions about benefit deadlines to outlining the performance review process. It also takes action on employees’ behalf — for example, it can schedule meetings, book meeting rooms, and provide internal job recommendations. The journey wasn’t a seamless, overnight flip of a switch. As part of our Customer Zero model, employees were the first users of Employee Agent, stress testers before the external launch. After one year of deployment, here’s what we’ve learned. 1. Define the problem to help define success. We developed Employee Agent in response to employee feedback that finding information was a barrier to productivity. At the same time, our employee support teams were inundated with inquiries. We structured the agent accordingly, and the success was clear: Today, organic employee adoption is past 80%. We also saw a 20% reduction in year-over-year case volume across 103,000 basic tier 1 cases. In particular, survey results also showed that employees wanted better visibility into career growth opportunities, so Employee Agent can now surface relevant open roles at Salesforce and provide personalized reskilling suggestions. It’s an important way of democratizing professional growth. Open Image Modal Image Modal 2. Make it easy and make it useful. To make navigation simple, we integrated Employee Agent directly into Slack — right where employees are already working. Having a single destination for employees made it much easier to find out which floor tech support is on in a specific office or get details about the midyear manager check-in process. Now employees can simply ask their agent in Slack to get the answer. Future iterations of Employee Agent will be folded into Slackbot along with other agents, strengthening Slack’s role as the front door to the employee experience. Employees also needed to be confident this tool was accurate, functional, and beneficial. So we focused on implementing capabilities that resulted in more frequent usage, such as tasks employees do on a regular basis like creating or summarizing meetings. Employee Agent also sends out proactive reminders around important events, reinforcing its function as a one-stop shop for all things HR. During open enrollment, we saw a 58% reduction in support cases, and employees had an 82% more positive experience compared with the previous year. That’s how you make an agent a go-to. Reduce the friction and make the employee experience easier. Invest in a solid data foundation. Being Customer Zero means being the first to determine what’s not working, and we quickly hit a humbling wall. An AI agent is only as smart as the data it can access, and our library of more than 1,000 internal knowledge articles was full of overlapping policies and outdated text. While humans could naturally filter through that noise, the agent took everything literally, resulting in confusing and sometimes incorrect answers. To overcome this, our teams conducted a complete overhaul of our knowledge ecosystem. We archived hundreds of outdated pages and streamlined complex policy language into clear, declarative data that agents could both understand and act on. We built it, broke it, fixed it, and shipped it again. That operational grit turned a chaotic text sprawl into a framework of trust that employees could rely on. Consolidating content was only half the story. We also rethought who we were writing for. We used to write short and concise articles so people could read them quickly and easily. Now we create robust, comprehensive knowledge articles so that agents have the full context they need. And we’re using AI to keep that knowledge base fresh and reliable so that the trust we built continues to hold up over time. AI scoring flags articles that need work, while case-data analysis spots missing knowledge context before it impacts performance. 4. Prioritize trust and compliance. Moving sensitive processes like HR functions to AI agents raises two natural concerns: confidentiality and compliance. Employees need to trust that every interaction stays confidential and follows company policy. Employee Agent keeps employee data safe with zero data retention, toxicity detection, and audit logging. Employee Agent now handles around 18,000 employee-verification cases a year, securely and compliantly. Employees no longer have to wait days to get letters verifying their employment at Salesforce for urgent life tasks like housing and loan applications. Another benefit of integrating the Employee Agent into the platform employees already use: The security and compliance protections are already there. The agent only shows employees information they are allowed to see based on their own individual permissions and guardrails. Looking ahead An agent can’t (and shouldn’t) do everything. Sensitive, high-stakes interactions, such as bereavement support or hiring decisions, remain in the purview of humans who have the relationship-building and emotional intelligence skills to navigate these complex situations. The key isn’t choosing between agents and humans — it’s building a strategy that leans into what each does best. Agents handle the agent work: the routine, the administrative, the always-on. Humans handle the human work: judgment, empathy, and relationships in the moments that matter most. The power is in how humans and agents work together, turning agents into a force multiplier for people — and for the Agentic Enterprise as a whole.
